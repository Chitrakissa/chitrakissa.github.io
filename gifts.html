A Rose, Written in Mathematics

The story behind it

It began with someone who deserved more than a rose from the shop on the corner. Real roses are beautiful for three days, then they droop on a windowsill and are forgotten. We wanted to give a rose that would still be there on the bad days, the far-away days, the days that needed one most.

So we made one that blooms the moment they open it, petal by petal, with their name drifting around it and a line of old Urdu poetry beneath. It never wilts. It opens a little differently every time. And months later, on an ordinary evening, they can open it again and feel exactly what they felt the first time: that someone thought they were worth something no shop sells.

"I couldn't find a rose good enough, so I wrote one for you."

What they receive

  • A rose that blooms as they watch, one they can turn in their fingers, which never wilts.
  • Their name drifting around it, with yours, and your own line beneath.
  • A couplet by Mir Taqi Mir, in Devanagari with an English rendering.
नाज़ुकी उस के लब की क्या कहिए पंखुड़ी इक गुलाब की सी है
How can I describe the softness of those lips? Like a single petal of a rose.
Mir Taqi Mir · 18th century

Two Stars

Riya & Kabir · Jaipur · a sample

Open the sample ↗

The story behind it

Two stars that pull each other into one shared orbit are the oldest picture of a couple there is. Each moves through its own life, unaware. Then, somehow, they come close enough to feel the pull, and they never quite let go.

We made the first one for two people living far apart, to tell them their own story in that language. The sky in it is the actual night sky above your city on the night you met, exactly as it was. They break a wax seal, watch two stars find each other, and read your own story as it unfolds, and for a minute the whole distance between you disappears.

"Until they locked together, like two stars, orbiting as one."

What they receive

  • A wax-sealed envelope, "For them, from you". Breaking the seal begins it.
  • Two stars that drift, feel the pull, and lock into one orbit, with your story told line by line.
  • The real sky of the place and night you met, reflected in a still lake.
