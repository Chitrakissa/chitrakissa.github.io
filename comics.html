Why a comic

Photos show a moment. A comic tells the story.

Every family has the story that gets told at every wedding: how they met, who said what, the auntie who knew first. It lives in a few people's memories and a hundred scattered photos, and a little of it is lost each time it's retold.

A comic keeps it whole. Panels let a story breathe: the glance across the platform, the umbrella that wouldn't open, the moment everyone at the table went quiet. Painted in warm watercolour, it becomes the thing people pick up at a party and pass around.

i.Your story, in your detailsThe station, the umbrella, the auntie who knew first: the details only you two know, drawn into your own story.
ii.Made to outlast the occasionFlowers fade in a week. A comic can be printed, framed, and picked up again years from now.
iii.It belongs only to youNo template couple and no stock faces: your city, your words, your story told the way you tell it.
